# Validation: is Proof-of-Done solving a real problem?

**Decide by Friday 24 October.** We need the answer before building the AI part. The submission deadline is 12 November.

## What desk research found (21 October)

**Late and unpaid work is common for freelancers.** These numbers come from secondary sources. Check the originals before quoting them in the video.

- Remote, *State of Freelance Work 2025*: 85% of freelancers are paid late at least some of the time, and 21% are paid late or not at all more than half the time ([via agiled.app](https://agiled.app/statistics/late-payment-statistics)).
- Bonsai, analysing 100,000+ freelancers: 29% of invoices are paid late (same source).
- Skynova: 48% of freelancers and business owners have been ghosted by a client ([Skynova](https://www.skynova.com/blog/late-invoice-payments); survey date unclear).

**In South Africa,** an informal poll of freelancers paid from abroad named payment disputes and delays as pain points ([ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/content/Kjlyr7wBNLZvk6am)). Withdrawing from PayPal to a South African bank has historically worked only through FNB ([Stuff, 2021](https://stuff.co.za/2021/03/16/paypal-in-africa-flutterwave/)). Check whether that has changed.

**So we must not claim to be the "first AI escrow".** Our angle is:

1. **Ordinary money through PayPal.** The client never touches crypto.
2. **Evidence, not opinion.** The AI turns the brief into checks. Real tools run those checks: pages load, the layout works on mobile, accessibility, speed, links, forms. The AI only explains the results.
3. **The client makes the final call.** The AI never moves money on its own.

## Riskiest assumptions (riskiest first)

1. **Clients will agree to a hold up front.** This is the biggest adoption risk. Why they might: a hold is gentler than a deposit, because nothing is charged until they approve.
2. **Freelancers' payment problems are about "is it done?" disputes,** not only slow or costly withdrawals. If it's mostly withdrawals, we're building the wrong product.
3. **Objective checks cover enough of a web deliverable** to settle real disagreements. Taste and design quality can't be automated; those stay with the client.
4. **PayPal's hold-then-capture works for a South African merchant account.** The spike answers this one (see the README).

## Interviews: 5 freelancers and 3 clients, 15 minutes each

Ask what already happened, not what people would do. "Would you use…?" gets polite yeses. Don't pitch until the end.

**Freelancers.** Try WSU classmates who freelance, East London designers and developers, and South African freelancer groups.

1. Tell me about the last project where getting paid took longer than you expected. What happened?
2. How did you and the client agree the work was finished? Was there any disagreement?
3. What do you do now to protect yourself: deposits, contracts, platforms? What does that cost you?
4. How do clients outside South Africa pay you? What's annoying about it?
5. If a client said "this isn't done", what evidence would you show them?

**Clients.** For example, the restaurant owners you build sites for.

1. The last time you paid someone for digital work, how did you pay: a deposit, in full, or on completion?
2. Have you ever paid for work that wasn't what you expected? What happened next?
3. How did you check the work was actually finished?
4. *At the end:* "If your money were held, not taken, until you approved the work, would you be comfortable with that? What would worry you?"

## Decision rule (Friday 24 October)

- **Go:** at least 3 of 5 freelancers describe a real payment or "is it done?" problem from the last 12 months without being prompted, and at least 2 of 3 clients are comfortable with a hold.
- **Adjust:** if the problems are mostly withdrawal fees and delays, refocus the pitch on getting paid across borders and keep the hold mechanic.
- **Stop:** if nobody has the problem, go back to the shortlist.
